ATS resume format

No plot twists: an ATS format resume reads top to bottom.

Applicant tracking systems read a resume like a text file: top to bottom, left to right. Our templates keep every section in a clear, logical order, without text boxes or floating elements that scramble your experience into nonsense. What a recruiter sees is exactly what the ATS reads.

ATS-friendly resume format

Fancy headings confuse bots. Our ATS resume templates don't.

An ATS sorts your resume into fields by looking for familiar headings. "Work Experience," "Education," and "Skills" land in the right place; "Where I've Made an Impact" might not land anywhere. Every template uses standard section headings by default, so your experience ends up where recruiters search for it.

Your frequently asked questions

What is an ATS resume?

An ATS resume is a resume formatted so that applicant tracking systems (ATS) can read it correctly. An ATS is software that employers use to collect, scan, and rank applications before a recruiter sees them. 99% of Fortune 500 companies use one, so if the software can't parse your resume, it may never reach a human.

What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

An ATS-friendly resume uses a clear, logical layout, standard section headings like "Work Experience" and "Education," real text instead of images, and standard fonts. It also includes the keywords and skills from the job ad. Every ATS resume template on this page has the formatting part built in, so you can focus on the content.

What is the ATS resume format?

The ATS resume format is a simple structure that applicant tracking systems can read from top to bottom without errors: clearly labeled sections, consistent dates, no text boxes or tables, and your contact details in the body of the resume. Want the details? Read our guide on how to format a resume.

How do I make my resume ATS-friendly?

Start with an ATS-friendly resume template, so the formatting is already right. Then tailor the content: mirror the keywords from the job ad, use standard section names, add dates to every entry, and spell out abbreviations at least once. Finally, run your resume through our ATS Resume Checker to catch anything you missed.

How do I know if my resume is ATS-friendly?

The quickest way is to test it. Upload your resume to our ATS Resume Checker: it simulates a real ATS scan, runs more than 20 checks, and gives you a score with a list of everything to fix.

Are there free ATS resume templates?

Yes. Box, one of the templates on this page, is completely free and just as ATS-friendly as the rest. You can find more in our free resume templates. Students and teachers can also get Kickresume Premium, including every ATS resume template, for free.

Can an ATS read a PDF resume?

Yes. Modern applicant tracking systems read PDF resumes without trouble, as long as the PDF contains real text rather than a scanned image. We regularly test our PDF resume templates with the most widely used ATS to make sure every field comes through intact.

Can an ATS-friendly resume have a photo?

An ATS can't read a photo; it simply skips it. In the US, UK, and Canada, it's best to leave the photo out, since recruiters there don't expect one. In countries where photos are standard, choose a template where the photo sits apart from your text, so it doesn't get in the way of parsing.

Which keywords should an ATS resume include?

Use the keywords from the job ad itself: the job title, required hard skills, tools, and certifications. Write them the way the employer does and work them naturally into your summary, experience, and skills sections. Our guide to resume keywords shows you how.

Do these templates work as an ATS-friendly CV?

Yes. Whether you call it a resume or a CV, an ATS reads it the same way. Every template on this page works as an ATS-friendly CV, so you can use it for applications anywhere in the world.
